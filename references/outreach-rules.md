# Outreach Rules — Voice, Volume and the Things That Reach a Person

Everything up to depth 4 is research. From depth 5 the run starts producing
things that land in front of a human being who built the company you are writing
about. The rules change character here, and they get stricter.

Two principles.

> **One serious person writing to another about their life's work.**
>
> **The volume that made the research work is the volume that ruins the
> outreach.**

---

## Part 1 — Volume discipline

A 2,000-row run creates the means to send 2,000 letters. Doing it is the single
worst thing you can do with this package.

Private segments are small towns. The owners sit on the same association board,
use the same bonding agent, drink at the same trade show, and forward things to
each other. A campaign that reads as a mail merge in one inbox reads as a mail
merge in forty, and the forty talk.

### The caps

| Cap | Default | Why |
|---|---|---|
| First touches per sender per week | **20** | What one person can follow up properly. Mandate section 11 sets it |
| Companies contacted in one metro per week | **5** | They know each other |
| Contacts per company | **1** | Messaging the owner and the owner's son in the same week reads as a merge to both |
| Live conversations at once | from mandate section 10 | The real constraint. Everything upstream is sized to it |
| Touches per company before stopping | **3** | Then dormant, with a revisit date |

The queue respects the caps even when there are more approved drafts than
capacity. Extra drafts wait a week. Nothing is sent to fill a quota.

### The batch overlap check

Before a batch is queued, diff the drafts against each other, sentence by
sentence.

If two messages going out in the same week share more than roughly **forty
percent** of their sentences, they are a template with the company name swapped,
and both get rewritten. Run the same check against the messages already sent in
the previous four weeks, because the owners who compare notes do not reset their
memory every Monday.

The honest version of this rule: if the only thing different between two letters
is the company name and the signal quote, you have written one letter twice.

### One row, one chance

A first touch is the only one that arrives with no history. Spending it on a row
where nothing specific is known, or on a plant manager who cannot sell, is not
a small waste. It is the row.

## Part 2 — What may be written at all

Drafting refuses, rather than improvising, when:

| Condition | What happens instead |
|---|---|
| Nothing specific and true is known about the company | Draft the honest categorical version, flag it as weaker, and say what research would fix it |
| The only personalization available is an inference about the owner as a person | **Refuse.** Age, health, family and wealth are not openers |
| The signal cannot be re-confirmed at its source URL | **Refuse** until the signal is re-verified or dropped |
| The row has `route_status` of `not-found` or `inferred-DO-NOT-USE` | No draft. There is nowhere for it to go |
| The company is on the suppression list in any scope | No draft, permanently |
| A retained banker is running a process and the mandate says do not go around | No draft. Write to the banker instead, and say so |
| The person identified cannot sell the company | No draft. Find the owner, or mark the row unroutable |
| `ownership_type` is `Not found` | No cold draft. A letter that guesses who owns the company announces itself |

**Refusing to write is a result.** It is reported at the gate with the reason,
and it is far cheaper than the alternative.

## Part 3 — Voice

### Length

| Format | Limit |
|---|---|
| First email | **under 120 words** |
| LinkedIn connect note | **under 300 characters** |
| Posted letter | one page, **under 250 words** |
| Any follow-up | shorter than the message before it |

If it does not fit, you are explaining instead of stating.

### Plain words

Short sentences, one idea each. Words you could say out loud across a counter
without sounding like a firm. The owner reads this on a phone between a customer
call and a staffing problem.

**No em dashes.** Use a period and start a new sentence. It is the most reliable
tell that a machine wrote the message.

### Say the thing

"I am looking to buy a [sector] business" beats every euphemism ever written.
Not "exploring strategic opportunities", not "a partnership conversation", not
"learning about your journey". The subject is ownership, and hiding it is what
offends. Owners respect directness and distrust everything else.

### Banned, without exception

> reaching out, touching base, circle back, quick call, pick your brain,
> exploring strategic alternatives, partnership opportunity, synergies, unlock
> value, take your business to the next level, exciting opportunity, I hope this
> email finds you well, I came across your company, impressed by your journey,
> leverage (as a verb), best-in-class, roll-up, platform (said to an owner),
> acquire (prefer "buy"), passionate about, delve, navigate, elevate, landscape,
> space (as in "the HVAC space"), monetize your life's work, at scale

Also out: any sentence beginning "As a [role]", and any compliment that could be
pasted onto a different company without editing.

### Never imply an offer or a valuation

No "businesses like yours are selling for X times earnings". No "we typically
pay between". You have not seen their numbers. A number in a first message is
either a fabrication or an anchor, and the owner knows which. If asked early,
the honest answer is that it depends on the financials and you would rather see
them than guess.

### No false familiarity and no invented warm paths

"I have long admired your business" when you found it on Tuesday. "A mutual
friend suggested I write" when none did. Owners in one industry know each other
and a fabricated warm path gets checked at the next association meeting.

A warm path exists only when it is recorded in the row **and** the person named
has agreed to be named. Otherwise write the honest cold version. It performs
better than a fake warm one, every time.

### No urgency and no pressure

"We are only looking at a few businesses this quarter." No. Selling a company is
the largest decision most owners ever make, and pressure in message one tells
them exactly how diligence will feel. The timeline is theirs.

### One ask, and make it small

The ask is never "sell". It is a short conversation, at their convenience, with
an explicit release valve: *if the timing is wrong, no reply needed and I will
not keep writing*.

## Part 4 — Structure of a first message

Four parts, in this order, nothing else.

1. **Who you are.** One sentence, plainly. Name, entity, and that you buy and
   hold businesses in their sector. Capital status exactly as mandate section 1
   permits
2. **Why this company.** One true, specific observation that could only be about
   them. Their licence history, their tenure, their niche, the thing they said in
   a trade piece. Where the row carries a quoted signal, this is where it goes
3. **The ask.** A short call or a reply. Nothing more
4. **The release valve.** One line that makes no-reply comfortable

No attachments on a first touch. No links except a plain one to who you are. No
signature essay. A postal address in the footer where the jurisdiction requires
one.

### Subject lines

The company's name, or the plain topic, or both: `[Company name]`,
`Buying a [sector] business in [region]`, `Question about [Company name]`.

Never clickbait, never blind, and never a fake thread. `Re:` on a first message
is a lie in four characters.

## Part 5 — Choosing the sender and the channel

**The sender.** Mandate section 11 says who may sign what and what each may
truthfully claim. Pick the sender whose true sentence is the most useful to this
specific owner. An operating partner who ran a company in the same trade
outranks a partner title every time, with owners who are still in the field. An
associate should almost never cold-write to an owner about selling their life's
work: the seniority mismatch reads as low priority, because it is.

**The channel**, in order of what actually works for this audience:

| Route in the row | Channel |
|---|---|
| Warm path recorded and cleared | An introduction. Not a cold message. Stop here |
| `verified` or `published` email | Email |
| Form or published phone only, no email | **Posted letter.** It is the least regulated channel in most jurisdictions and frequently the most effective with an offline owner |
| LinkedIn URL only | Connect note, under 300 characters, from a senior sender only |
| Nothing | Unroutable. Say so. Do not guess an address |

A posted letter to a sixty-two-year-old owner of a licensed trade business
outperforms a cold email most of the time, and almost nobody sends one.

---

## Part 6 — Follow-ups

- Each one **shorter** than the last
- Each adds **one new true thing**. Never "just bumping this"
- Never guilt. No "I noticed you have not replied"
- Three touches over four to six weeks, then stop
- A non-reply is not a no. Move the row to dormant with a revisit date and leave
  the relationship undamaged. Owners sell on their own clock, sometimes years
  after the first letter
- An opt-out is permanent, on every channel, with no clever workaround

## Part 7 — The checks every draft carries

Recorded in `checks_passed` in `outreach.csv`, so the approval gate shows the
work rather than asserting it.

- [ ] Word or character count inside the limit, with the number stated
- [ ] No banned word or phrase
- [ ] No em dash
- [ ] Every claim about the sender true per mandate section 11
- [ ] Every claim about the company sourced from the row, with the field named
- [ ] Any quote verbatim, re-confirmed at its URL, with the date
- [ ] No valuation, multiple, offer or price implied
- [ ] No fabricated warm path, no named third party without consent
- [ ] No urgency, no scarcity, no pressure
- [ ] Four-part structure, one ask, release valve present
- [ ] Opt-out line present where the channel requires it
- [ ] Postal address present where the jurisdiction requires it
- [ ] Not on the suppression list, re-checked at queue time
- [ ] Batch overlap under 40% against this batch and the last four weeks
- [ ] Read aloud without running out of breath

### The two tests that catch more than the checklist

**The read-aloud test.** If you would not say it to this owner across their own
counter, rewrite it. If you run out of breath in a sentence, cut it.

**The owner test.** Would *you* reply to this, if it were about your company? Not
"is it polite". Would you reply. If the honest answer is no, it does not go in
the queue.

---

## Part 8 — Nothing in this package sends

Depth 7 stages a queue. A human reads it and sends it by hand, or exports it and
sends it from their own tool after reading every line.

There is no send step, no integration and no automation hook, and that is a
design decision rather than a missing feature. An automatic send loop attached to
a language model, a list of two thousand named owners and a regulated activity is
a bad idea in every jurisdiction, and the first time it goes wrong it goes wrong
at volume.
