# Owner Outreach Drafting

Depth 6. Everything before this was research. This is a letter to a person who
built the company you have been reading about.

**Read first, every time:** `references/outreach-rules.md`. It is the rules and
this skill does not override any of them. Also `MANDATE.md` sections 1, 11 and
12, which say who may sign, what they may truthfully claim, and which channels
and jurisdictions are in play.

> One serious person writing to another about their life's work.

---

## Step 0 — Preconditions, and the refusals

In scope: rows at `depth` = `5` with `route_status` of `verified`, `published` or
`form-or-phone`. Warm-path rows are introductions, not drafts — hand them to the
user with a one-paragraph brief and stop.

Before writing a single message, check the refusals. **Refusing is a result**,
reported with its reason, and it is far cheaper than the alternative.

| Condition | What happens |
|---|---|
| Nothing specific and true is known about the company | Draft the honest categorical version, flag it as weaker, say what research would fix it |
| The only personalization available is an inference about the owner as a person | **Refuse.** Age, health, family and wealth are not openers |
| The signal cannot be re-confirmed at its source URL **today** | **Refuse** until the signal is re-verified or dropped |
| `ownership_type` is `Not found` | **Refuse.** A letter that guesses who owns the company announces itself |
| On `suppression.csv` in any scope | **Refuse**, permanently |
| A retained banker is running a process and the mandate says do not go around | **Refuse.** Offer a letter to the banker instead |
| Mandate section 11 is empty | **Stop the whole depth.** There is no sender, so there is nobody for the message to be true about |

### Re-verify before you quote

Open the `signal_source` URL. If the page no longer says it, the quote does not
go in a letter. This is the second check on the same fact — the audit did the
first — because a fabricated or stale quote is the one error in this package that
reaches an owner, and it arrives in writing with their name at the top.

---

## Step 1 — Pick the sender

From mandate section 11. Pick the sender whose **true sentence** is the most
useful to this specific owner.

An operating partner who ran a company in the same trade outranks a partner
title every time with an owner who is still in the field. A fund partner is right
where the conversation is about capital and continuity. An associate should
almost never cold-write to an owner about selling their life's work: the
seniority mismatch reads as low priority, because it is.

Every claim in the message must be true about the person signing it. Capital
status exactly as section 1 permits, and nothing beyond it.

---

## Step 2 — Write the first touch

Four parts, in this order, nothing else.

1. **Who you are.** One sentence, plainly
2. **Why this company.** One true, specific observation that could only be about
   them. This is where the row's quoted signal goes, or the licence history, or
   the tenure, or the niche
3. **The ask.** A short call or a reply. Nothing more
4. **The release valve.** One line that makes no-reply comfortable

Limits: email under 120 words. Letter one page, under 250 words. LinkedIn note
under 300 characters. Follow-ups shorter than the message before.

### A first email, worked

> **Subject:** Northline Electric
>
> Mr Sandoval, my name is Ray Okoye. I ran a 200-person electrical contractor in
> Birmingham for eleven years and sold it in 2019. I now buy electrical and
> building systems businesses in the Southeast with my own fund, and we hold
> them. We are not brokers.
>
> You told the trade press in June that service contracts are now half the work
> and that neither of your children went into the trade. That is the part I would
> want to talk about. Not the numbers.
>
> Would you be open to a short call in the next few weeks, whenever suits you?
>
> If the timing is wrong, no reply is needed and I will not keep writing.
>
> Ray Okoye
> Kestrel Lane
> 1 Example Plaza, Suite 400, Atlanta, GA 30309

104 words. The second paragraph could not have been written about any other
company, and every word of it came out of the row.

### A letter, worked

Same four parts, more room, posted. Use it for the `form-or-phone` rows.

> Dear Mr Hollis,
>
> My name is Ray Okoye. I ran an electrical contracting business in Birmingham
> for eleven years, about the size yours is now, and sold it in 2019. I now buy
> and hold electrical and building systems companies in the Southeast with my own
> fund. We are not brokers and we are not raising money.
>
> I am writing because of what your licence record shows. Hollis Electric has
> held the same master licence since 1986 and the same two officers since 1994,
> which in this trade usually means a company that has been run carefully and has
> never had to be sold.
>
> I am not asking you to sell anything. I am asking whether you would spend half
> an hour on the phone with somebody who has done the job, so that if you ever do
> think about what happens next, you already know one buyer who is not a broker
> and not a fund with a clock on it.
>
> If you would rather not, no reply is needed and I will not write again.
>
> Yours sincerely,
> Ray Okoye

210 words. No signal existed on this row at all, and the specificity came from a
free Tier 1 licence record instead.

### A LinkedIn note, worked

> Mr Sandoval, I ran an electrical contractor in Birmingham for 11 years and
> sold it in 2019. I now buy and hold electrical businesses in the Southeast.
> Saw your piece on the service side of the business. Would you be open to a
> short call sometime? If not, no problem at all.

277 characters.

## Step 3 — Write the two follow-ups

Three touches total, over four to six weeks, then stop.

- Each **shorter** than the one before
- Each adds **one new true thing**. Never "just bumping this"
- Never guilt. No "I noticed you have not replied"
- Never a new ask. The ask stays the same and stays small

Follow-up one, worked:

> Mr Sandoval, one thing I should have said. When I sold my business the part I
> got wrong was waiting until I had to sell rather than talking to buyers while I
> still had the choice. That is the only reason I write to people who are not
> selling.
>
> Still happy to talk whenever suits. If not, I will leave it there.

Follow-up two, worked:

> Mr Sandoval, I will stop here so I am not a nuisance. If anything changes, my
> number is below and it will still be me who answers.

Then the row goes dormant with a revisit date. A non-reply is not a no. Owners
sell on their own clock, sometimes years after the first letter, and the
relationship is worth more than the sequence.

---

## Step 4 — The batch overlap check

Before the batch is handed over, diff the drafts against each other, sentence by
sentence. Then diff them against everything sent in the previous four weeks.

**Over roughly 40% sentence overlap between two messages going out the same week,
both get rewritten.** The owners who compare notes sit on the same association
board and they do not reset their memory every Monday.

The honest version: if the only difference between two letters is the company
name and the quote, you have written one letter twice.

Report the overlap numbers at the gate. They are a number, so they can be
checked.

---

## Step 5 — The checks, recorded on every draft

Write them into `checks_passed` in `outreach.csv` so the gate shows the work
rather than asserting it.

- [ ] Word or character count inside the limit, number stated
- [ ] No banned word or phrase from `references/outreach-rules.md`
- [ ] No em dash
- [ ] Every claim about the sender true per mandate section 11
- [ ] Every claim about the company sourced from the row, with the field named
- [ ] Any quote verbatim and re-confirmed at its URL today, with the date
- [ ] No valuation, multiple, offer or price implied
- [ ] No fabricated warm path, no third party named without consent
- [ ] No urgency, no scarcity, no pressure
- [ ] Four-part structure, one ask, release valve present
- [ ] Opt-out line present where the channel requires it
- [ ] Postal address present where the jurisdiction requires it
- [ ] Batch overlap under 40%
- [ ] Read aloud without running out of breath
- [ ] The owner test: would you reply to this, if it were about your company

---

## Step 6 — The gate

### 🚧 HARD GATE

Show **every draft in full**, not a sample, with:

- The sender and why that sender
- The personalization, and the row field it came from
- The checks, including the overlap numbers
- Every refusal, with its reason and what would fix it

Get approval. Then write to `outreach.csv` with `status` = `draft`, and set
`depth` = `6`.

Nothing is queued by this skill and nothing is sent by this package.

## Notes

- **A thin row makes a short letter, not a padded one.** The categorical version
  said plainly beats a specific-sounding sentence that is not quite true
- **Never put a number in a first message.** No valuation, no multiple, no
  "businesses like yours". You have not seen their numbers
- **The release valve is not politeness.** It is the line that makes a no-reply
  comfortable, which is what keeps the relationship intact for the second
  conversation in two years
- **Read every draft aloud.** If you would not say it across their counter,
  rewrite it
- **Nothing in this package sends.** A human reads the queue and sends it by
  hand
